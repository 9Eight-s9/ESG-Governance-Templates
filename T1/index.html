📌 About This Instrument

The ESG Materiality Assessment Matrix (B.1) is a structured tool for identifying and prioritising the environmental, social, and governance issues most significant to your organisation. It combines external stakeholder perspective with internal financial significance to produce a defensible, board-ready materiality determination.

This instrument covers 35 topics across three pillars — 13 Environmental, 12 Social, 10 Governance — and is applicable across all industries. Each topic includes description, industry guidance, and framework references.

📐 The Scoring Formula — Explained
Combined Score = (SI × 0.55) + (BS × 0.45)
Materiality Index = Σ(Combined Scores) ÷ (N × 5) × 100
SI — Stakeholder Impact 55% weight

Measures how significantly this ESG topic affects external stakeholders — communities, investors, employees, regulators, customers, and civil society. Weighted at 55% because ESG originated as a stakeholder-facing discipline. This weight satisfies the impact materiality dimension of CSRD/ESRS and GRI double materiality.

BS — Business Financial Significance 45% weight

Measures how significantly this topic affects the company's financial position, results of operations, or long-term enterprise value. Weighted at 45% to capture the investor-centric financial materiality required by ISSB IFRS S1, BRSR Core, and TCFD — ensuring the tool works for all major frameworks.

🎚 Scoring Scale Reference
Stakeholder Impact (SI) — External Perspective
1
Insignificant — No meaningful stakeholder concern. Negligible or no external impact on communities, environment, or workers. Not on any investor or NGO radar.
2
Minor — Some awareness among a small stakeholder group. Limited media attention. Topic is manageable with standard business practice.
3
Moderate — Raised regularly by investors, employees, or NGOs. Sector peers are disclosing on this topic. Active regulatory interest exists.
4
Significant — Top priority for key investors or customers. Active regulatory scrutiny. Material risk of media attention, investor engagement, or contract loss if unaddressed.
5
Critical — Existential stakeholder concern. Active regulation or litigation. A failure here could trigger licence withdrawal, boycott, or supply chain disruption.
Business Financial Significance (BS) — Internal Perspective
1
Negligible — No foreseeable financial consequence. Does not affect revenue, cost, risk exposure, or access to capital in any foreseeable scenario.
2
Limited — Minor cost implication. Would not appear in a risk register. Financial impact below 0.5% of EBITDA in a reasonable scenario.
3
Moderate — Impacts operating cost, talent attraction, or financing terms. Would appear in management discussion. 0.5–2% EBITDA impact range.
4
Material — A named principal risk in the board risk register. Affects investor confidence, cost of capital, or regulatory compliance cost. 2–5% EBITDA impact.
5
Critical — Could threaten the business model or trigger material regulatory enforcement. Financial impact exceeding 5% of EBITDA or existential in nature.
🔢 How to Complete the Assessment — 6 Steps
1
Fill Organisation Details on the Dashboard tab. This appears in exports and printed reports.
2
Read guidance on this tab until you understand the two axes and the scoring scale.
3
Score each topic in the Environmental, Social, and Governance tabs. Use the dual sliders — SI and BS independently. Click "Show framework guidance" on any topic for regulatory context.
4
Document rationale in the Notes field for every topic scored 3 or above. Assurance providers will request this evidence.
5
Return to Dashboard to review the materiality matrix, priority ranking, and interpretation.
6
Export JSON to save your session. Import it later to resume. Print / PDF for board submission. Every page load starts blank — your data is never stored on a server.
