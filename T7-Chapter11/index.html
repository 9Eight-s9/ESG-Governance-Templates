Chapter 11 · Phase 1 · Baselining, Materiality & Programme Design

ESG Phase 1 Implementation Toolkit

This toolkit operationalises Chapter 11 of ESG Mastery. Phase 1 is the foundation of the entire ESG programme — the work done before any targets are set, before any reports are published, and before any assurance is obtained. The sequence is non-negotiable: materiality first, then baseline, then governance, then reporting.

🎯
Why Phase 1 Matters

The most common and most damaging ESG mistake is starting with the report rather than the assessment. Companies that decide what they want to say, then look for data to support it, produce disclosure that cannot survive regulatory scrutiny or investor due diligence. The correct sequence — required by CSRD, ISSB IFRS S1, BRSR Core, and GRI — is to start with understanding, not communication.

A rigorous Phase 1 baseline is the foundation of all future credibility. Ørsted's extraordinary transformation from one of Europe's most carbon-intensive utilities to a global leader in offshore wind rested on a specific, measurable, independently auditable starting point: 16 million tCO₂e in 2006. Without that baseline, none of the subsequent reduction claims would have been verifiable.

🔢
The Mandated Sequence
1
Programme Charter — Board-approved document defining scope, frameworks, ownership, timeline
2
Materiality Assessment — Stakeholder-informed prioritisation of which ESG topics to report and manage
3
Emissions Baseline — Scope 1, 2, and 3 calculation per GHG Protocol in chosen base year
4

5
Governance Design — Board committee, management structure, ESG-linked pay (Chapter 12)
6
Target Setting — SBTi, net-zero, water, biodiversity targets grounded in baseline
7
First Disclosure — Report against material topics with assured baseline data (Chapter 14)

Part B — Reporting Boundary Mandatory · GHG Protocol decision
GHG Protocol Boundary Approaches: Operational Control — report all sources over which the company has the authority to introduce and implement operating policies. Most common for ESG reporting. Financial Control — report all sources where the company has the ability to direct financial and operating policies. Equity Share — report emissions proportional to ownership share. Each approach gives a different boundary — this decision affects every Scope 1 and 2 figure and must be applied consistently.

Part E — SBTi & Net-Zero Commitment Nice to Have — World-Class
Science Based Targets initiative (SBTi): The SBTi Corporate Net-Zero Standard requires companies to set near-term targets (2030) aligned to 1.5°C pathways and long-term net-zero targets (2050). Targets must be validated before being made public. SBTi validation requires a GHG Protocol-aligned baseline — this toolkit builds that baseline. Committing to SBTi in the Programme Charter signals ambition to investors and clients before targets are validated.
🌍 World-Class Programme Charter — What Global Leaders Include
Ørsted (Denmark) — Charter included explicit carbon intensity reduction KPI (tCO₂e/MWh) tied to executive pay from Year 1 of programme. Board ESG Committee had direct line to CEO, not filtered through sustainability function.
Unilever (UK/Netherlands) — USLP Charter committed to double materiality before CSRD existed. Included social impact targets (livelihoods, health, gender equality) alongside environmental ones — recognising that investor materiality and stakeholder materiality overlap.
Microsoft (US) — Carbon negative by 2030 Charter included Scope 3 Category 11 (use of sold products) — the emissions from customers running Azure — committing to a target that most companies exclude. Also committed to restoring more water than consumed by 2030.
Mahindra (India) — BRSR Core Charter included value chain coverage commitment: ESG assessments for suppliers covering 67% of procurement spend, pre-empting regulatory requirement by 2 years. SBTi commitment made public before targets were validated.

🇪🇺 Double Materiality (CSRD/ESRS/GRI)

Assess BOTH: (1) How ESG issues affect the company financially, AND (2) How the company's activities impact people and the environment — regardless of financial feedback. Both dimensions create reporting obligations.

🌍 Single / Financial Materiality (ISSB/BRSR/SEC)

Assess: Does this ESG issue affect the company's financial performance, cash flows, access to capital, or value? If a reasonable investor would want to know it, it is material. Environmental or social impacts with no financial feedback are not required to be reported.

Step 4 — Common Failure Modes to Avoid
⚠️
Insufficient Stakeholder Reach

Engaging only senior managers and one or two investors misses the perspectives of the people most directly affected. Community voices, supply chain workers, and frontline employees surface issues that management does not see.

⚠️
Pre-Determined Outcomes

If the materiality assessment confirms exactly what management already believed without surprising anyone, the process was probably not rigorous. A good assessment should produce at least one result that challenges internal assumptions.

⚠️
Treating the Matrix as Permanent

Dynamic materiality means issues move. GDPR made data privacy financially material within 5 years. Office circular economy moved from facilities management to ESRS E5 obligation. Reassess annually — at minimum review the medium-priority band.

⚠️
No Board Validation

Under CSRD, ISSB S1, and BRSR Core, board sign-off is a governance requirement, not a formality. The materiality determination commits the company to reporting obligations and risk accountability — the board must own that commitment.

🔥 Scope 1 Baseline
Direct Emissions — Base Year Calculation
GHG Protocol Corporate Standard · DEFRA / EPA / BEE emission factors · tCO₂e = Activity × EF
— tCO₂e
Methodology: tCO₂e = Activity Data × Emission Factor. GWP100 values: IPCC AR6 (CO₂=1, CH₄=27.9, N₂O=273). Use DEFRA (UK), EPA eGRID (US), or BEE/CEA (India) emission factors. All Scope 1 is location-based — no market-based adjustment applies. Record data quality: Measured (metered) / Calculated (engineering estimate) / Estimated (spend-based proxy).

Purchased Energy Emissions — Dual Reporting (Location & Market-Based)
GHG Protocol Scope 2 Guidance (2015) · ESRS E1 · CDP C8 · SBTi uses market-based
— tCO₂e (LB)
Dual reporting is mandatory under GHG Protocol: (1) Location-based — uses national/regional grid average emission factor (DEFRA, IEA, CEA). (2) Market-based — uses contractual EF from RECs, GOs, PPAs, or supplier-specific data. Zero if 100% renewable verified. SBTi target validation uses market-based. CDP and ESRS E1 require both. Never use grid average as market-based.

Data Governance — Four Required Components Mandatory for Assured Disclosure
ESRS, BRSR Core, and ISSB-aligned frameworks all require assured ESG disclosure. Assurance providers (Big 4 and specialist firms) review: data ownership records, collection methodology documentation, review and approval hierarchy, and system audit trails. Building this infrastructure in Phase 1 costs a fraction of retrofitting it when an assurance provider flags data quality gaps.
1
Data Ownership

Every ESG data point must have a named owner — a specific individual responsible for accuracy, timeliness, and completeness.

2
Data Collection Calendar

ESG data must be collected on a scheduled basis with the same discipline as financial close.

3
Review & Approval Hierarchy

Three-level review ensures data quality before assurance engagement.

L1
Data Owner validates at source — checks for completeness, reasonableness, and consistency with prior periods. Flags anomalies.
↓
L2
Function ESG Data Coordinator reviews — cross-checks against utility bills, invoices, travel booking system. Signs off on function submission.
↓
L3
ESG Programme Lead approves — consolidates all functions, conducts cross-pillar reasonableness check, submits to assurance engagement.
4
Documentation Standard

Every material data point requires methodology documentation for assurance review.
